Design Sprint Agencies in the UK: How to Compare Providers

Dr Andrew Greenland • 15 October 2026 • 5 min read

Short answer: when comparing design sprint providers in the UK, look at five things: who actually facilitates on the day, whether they will run the sprint with your team rather than for it, how they recruit and run user tests, what you have in writing at the end, and the total price for the whole sprint. Expect to pay roughly £10,000 to £16,000 for a facilitated three to five day sprint from a specialist, and more if the provider also builds the product afterwards.

Design sprints have become a popular way to test a new product, service or process before committing serious money to it. That popularity means the label now covers very different offers, from a single facilitator running your team through the process to agencies that run the sprint themselves and then sell you the build. This guide explains the main types of provider and how to compare them.

What you are buying

A design sprint takes one well-defined challenge through five stages - map, sketch, decide, prototype and test - usually in three to five days. The outputs should be a realistic prototype, feedback from around five real users, and a clear decision about whether to go ahead, stop or change direction.

Most of the value comes from two things a provider brings: a facilitator who keeps a mixed group moving and stops the most senior voice from dominating, and a disciplined testing day that produces evidence rather than opinion.

Five questions to compare providers

  1. Who facilitates on the day? The person who sells the sprint is not always the person who runs it. Ask for the facilitator's name and background.
  2. Will our team do the work? If the provider's own designers do most of the thinking, your team learns less and may not own the outcome.
  3. How are user tests run? Ask who recruits the five test users, who interviews them and how the findings are recorded. Weak testing turns a sprint into an expensive brainstorm.
  4. What do we have in writing at the end? Look for the prototype, recorded test findings and a written decision with next steps and owners.
  5. What is the total price? Ask for a price for the whole sprint, including preparation and follow-up, not just a day rate.

What a design sprint costs in the UK

Prices vary with length, team size and whether the provider also designs or builds. As a guide:

  • a facilitated three to five day sprint from a specialist typically costs around £10,000 to £16,000 for the whole team
  • agency sprints that include design and prototype production by the agency's own staff usually cost more
  • training courses for staff are priced per person and are a different product

When a design sprint is the wrong choice

A design sprint assumes the team agrees on the problem and that one person can decide. If your leaders disagree about what the problem is, or it is unclear who has the authority to choose, a sprint will stall in the first morning. In that case, start with a short decision workshop to agree priorities and decision rights, then sprint on what comes out of it. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a design sprint cost in the UK?

A facilitated three to five day design sprint from a specialist typically costs around £10,000 to £16,000 for the whole team. Agency sprints that include design and build work by the agency usually cost more.

How do I choose a design sprint agency?

Ask who facilitates on the day, whether your team does the work, how user tests are run, what you will have in writing at the end and what the total price is. These five answers separate a useful sprint from an expensive brainstorm.

Should we hire an agency or an independent facilitator for a design sprint?

Hire an agency if you want them to design and build the product afterwards. Hire an independent facilitator if you want your own team to do the work and own the result.

How long does a design sprint take?

Usually five days, or three days in a compressed format for teams that have already defined the challenge well.

What should a design sprint produce?

A realistic prototype, feedback from around five real users, and a written decision to go ahead, stop or change direction, with next steps and owners.
