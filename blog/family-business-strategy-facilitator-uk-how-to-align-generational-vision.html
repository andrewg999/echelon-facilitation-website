The RACI Framework in a Family Business Context

The RACI framework (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) provides the necessary clarity to separate family roles from professional duties. It ensures that only one person holds the weight of a decision. A practical application might look like this:

  • Capital Expenditure: The CFO is Accountable; the Managing Director is Responsible; the Family Board is Consulted.
  • Brand Strategy: The Marketing Lead is Accountable; the Founder is Consulted; the wider family is Informed.

Defining these roles prevents the common deadlock where multiple family members believe they have a veto. It allows the business to move at the speed of the market rather than the speed of family agreement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does a family business strategy facilitator differ from a coach?

Facilitation prioritises collective leadership alignment on strategic objectives rather than individual development. Whilst coaching focuses on personal performance, a facilitator manages group dynamics to reach a specific commercial outcome. This professional intervention ensures the board makes decisions based on objective data rather than interpersonal history. It shifts the focus from individual accolades to the success of the entire organisation.

Why do UK family firms struggle with strategic alignment?

UK family firms frequently struggle with alignment because they lack a disciplined framework to separate family dynamics from business execution. Decision-making often stalls due to the fear that professional challenge will cause personal friction amongst relatives. Without objective distance, subjective narratives often override commercial reality. This leads to strategic paralysis that prevents the firm from adapting to market shifts or regulatory changes.

What happens during a family business strategy offsite?

A strategy offsite provides a neutral ground to challenge legacy assumptions and define a unified vision for the 2026/2027 cycle. The session involves a structured review of market data, a Decision-Rights Reset, and the creation of an actionable execution plan. Engaging a family business strategy facilitator uk ensures the discussion remains focused on high-stakes objectives rather than historical grievances or emotional narratives.

Should non-family directors attend strategy facilitation sessions?

Non-family directors should attend to ensure the strategy is grounded in operational reality and objective truth. Their participation provides a necessary counter-balance to family-centric perspectives and reinforces a professionalised boardroom culture. Excluding them risks creating a "shadow board" dynamic where critical decisions are made in isolation. Inclusion ensures that those responsible for execution are fully aligned with the strategic direction.
