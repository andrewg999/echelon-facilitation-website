Pipeline Test: Why Every Leadership Team Needs a Decision Framework

Dr Andrew Greenland • 30 March 2026 • 1 min read

The Decision Gap

Most leadership teams spend 80% of their meeting time discussing issues and only 20% making actual decisions. This imbalance creates a dangerous decision gap that compounds over time, leading to strategic drift and missed opportunities.

Three Pillars of Effective Decision-Making

After facilitating over 200 executive workshops, three non-negotiable pillars emerge consistently:

1. Clarity of Options

Before any decision can be made, the team must have complete clarity on what options are actually available. This means moving beyond the obvious binary choices and exploring the full spectrum of possibilities.

2. Transparent Trade-offs

Every strategic decision involves trade-offs. The most effective teams are those who surface these trade-offs explicitly rather than allowing them to remain hidden assumptions.

3. Committed Action

A decision without committed action is merely a preference. The final pillar requires each team member to articulate their specific commitments and accountability measures.

Putting It Into Practice

The next time your leadership team faces a critical strategic decision, try structuring the conversation around these three pillars. You may be surprised at how quickly alignment emerges when the framework is clear.
